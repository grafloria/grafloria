The Tab Group Drop Map

Every point over the fluid board’s side panel, sampled with an independent drag of the NPS widget, reading the kit’s own decision rather than guessing from pixels. 1,040 points on a 22 px lattice, then one-pixel sweeps across every boundary. Both defects it found are now fixed and live.

The tab header is a clean 30 pixel target and it lands exactly where it is painted. Everything else about the panel’s top was wrong, in two separate ways that compounded.

Both were reported from use, and both were measured rather than argued. The first was a design choice that had outgrown its container: a fifth of a panel’s body meant “put this above the whole panel”, and on a panel this tall a fifth is 216 pixels of what looks like content. The second was the cost of the fix that stopped the flicker: the tab target is pinned to where the panel rests, so once the panel had been shoved aside, the tabs you could see were not the tabs you could hit.

They shared one cause — the preview moved the thing you were aiming at — and both are fixed. 0.4.61 stopped the vertical bands moving the panel at all until you let go. 0.4.62 made those bands a fixed depth instead of a fifth. That left a third problem, raised immediately: a fixed depth is a good target and an impossible guess, since to put a widget above a panel you point just below its header. So 0.4.63 paints the bands while you drag. The map below is drawn as the panel behaves now, with the old boundaries marked where they were.

The header, unchanged

It accepts a drop across its full width and its full 30 pixel height, and not one pixel outside. Target and paint agree.

Problem one, fixed in 0.4.62

216 px under the tabs meant “above the panel”. It is 30 px now, and the body is 59% “into the page” instead of 36%.

Problem two, fixed in 0.4.61

Using that band shoved the panel 140 px and the tab target did not follow. The panel no longer moves until you release.

What every point means

  • A new tab. The widget joins the strip at the slot under the pointer. The panel does not move.
  • Into the page. The widget is adopted by whichever tab page is showing, beside what that page already holds.
  • The panel gives way. The widget takes a cell on the board next to the panel — above, below, left or right. Above and below are marked while you hold and applied on release; left and right still slide live, because sideways the header stays under your hand.
  • A plain board cell. Nothing to do with the panel.
  • And you can see all of it. Since 0.4.63 the four bands are shaded while a widget is held over the container, with the clear middle meaning the page, so none of this has to be guessed.

Six findings, as measured on 0.4.60

  1. The header is honest, and that is new

    A one-pixel vertical sweep puts the tab zone at y 113 to 142 — exactly the painted strip, no more and no less — across the panel’s whole width. Once your hand is on it, it holds for nine pixels past the bottom edge. Nothing here needs fixing.

  2. A fifth of the body means “above the whole panel”

    Every container edge claims the outer fifth of the body for “next to it”. On a panel 1,110 px tall that is 216 px, and it starts immediately under the tabs. So the top fifth of what reads as page content is not page content at all. The band is a fraction, so it grows with the panel: a short tab group 300 px tall would claim only 54 px.

  3. Using it shoves the panel a whole row

    Dropping above the panel pushes it down 140 px — the widget’s own height plus the gap — and the board grows from 1,130 to 1,260 px to hold it. That is the intended outcome. It is just very large for something you reach by aiming ten pixels below the tabs.

  4. The tab target never follows the panel

    Enter the content area 30 px under the tabs and the panel drops 140 px, so the tabs are now painted at y 253. The tab zone stays at 113–142. Coming back up, the drop does not become a tab until y 142 — 111 px above the tabs you can see, in what is now empty space.

  5. Chasing the visible tabs pushes them further

    Move down onto the tabs where they now sit and you are still in the band, one row lower, so the panel is pushed another 140 px — to y 393. The tabs run away from the hand following them, 140 px at a time.

  6. A section does not behave like this

    The Operations section takes a widget anywhere in its body below its caption band. It has no edge bands at all. Only tab groups have the fifths, so two containers on the same board answer the same gesture differently.

The journey, in three frames

Why the target stopped following

This is worth stating plainly, because it is a trade I made last night. Until 0.4.60 the strip was found by hit-testing the box it is painted in. That box travels with the panel, so it swept through a motionless pointer, claimed the hand as a tab, brought the panel home, handed the hand straight back to the band, and went round again. A hand that had stopped moving saw the answer change 35 times in one slow crossing.

Reading the strip where the panel rests ended that loop, and it is the same rule a dragged section has used since 0.4.44. But it only holds while the panel stays put. The moment the band pushes the panel, resting position and painted position part company, and finding 4 is the gap between them. Both problems have one root: the preview moves the thing you are aiming at.

What changed

The first two shipped, in this order. The third was the simpler alternative and was not taken.

Shipped — element 0.4.61

The panel does not move until you release

The cell the widget will take is marked with an overlay and the panel stays where it is, exactly as a tab drag has done since 0.4.42 — the kit learned this lesson once, for the other gesture, after a page could not be put back into its own panel. The header never moves now, resting and painted position are always the same, and findings 4 and 5 are gone. The horizontal shift at the board’s edge still slides live: it moves the header sideways under a full-width target, so it never leaves the hand. Two scopes the lab insisted on: a dragged section keeps its live push, which is intent rather than preview, and a palette chip applies its mark in its own release path.

Shipped — element 0.4.62

A tab group’s top and bottom bands become a fixed depth

Not a fifth of the body but the strip’s own height, 30 px. The content area now means “into the page” almost everywhere, and “above the panel” is a thin lane under the tabs. The left and right fifths stayed: they are how a widget gets beside a full-height panel, and nobody has reported them surprising. The lab found one rule on the way — a beside that is only marked has no vacated cell and must not borrow the one it would take, because for a widget the size of its container that rect is the whole panel.

Shipped — element 0.4.63

And the bands are painted, so they can be aimed at

A fixed depth solved the surprise and created a new one: nobody would guess that the way to put a widget above a panel is to point just below its header. While a widget is held over a container its four bands are now shaded, and the clear middle is the page. The band your hand is in still wears the dashed outline of the cell it will take. This only became possible once the container stopped moving, since a lane that slides away is not a lane you can aim at. The lanes are computed from the same tree and the same frames the drop decision uses, so a painted lane and the zone it stands for cannot disagree.

Not taken

A tab group loses its top and bottom bands entirely

Its body would mean “into the page”, full stop, matching the section beside it on the same board, and closing finding 6 as well. The cost is that a panel occupying the full height of its column could no longer be given a neighbour above or below it — there is no board row left to point at — so the gesture would be lost rather than moved. Finding 6 therefore stands: a section still has no bands at all.

Measured on grafloria.com and on a local build of the same commit, Chromium 1440 × 1400, light and dark. Each sample is an independent gesture: press, one pointer move to the target, read, Escape. Sampling by continuous sweep was tried first and thrown away — the intermediate pointer positions set zones of their own on the way, and the map it drew was wrong.

Then tested exhaustively, on the deployed build. 252 real drops on a 44 × 62 px lattice covering the panel, its margins and the board around it. At every point: hold, read what the kit shows, release, measure what it did, undo, and check the board came back byte for byte. What was shown is what happened at all 252, with no overlap left behind, no undo that failed, and no page error. Every outcome that pushes the panel was shown before it happened, and the lanes appeared on exactly the 144 points over the container and on none of the 108 outside it.
