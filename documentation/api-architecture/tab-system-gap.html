Tabs: what VS Code does, what the kit does

Every row below was measured against element 0.4.27, not assumed; the rows marked FIXED or HAVE in 0.4.29 were re-measured after the change. The verdicts come from driving a real browser: a probe page with eight tabs, a mixed container, a tab container on a split board, tabs nested inside a tab page, a static board, and a widget dragged in and out of an open page.

The model, side by side

VS Code's editor area is a split tree of groups, and each group is a tab bar over one visible editor. The kit has both halves already: layout: 'split' is the tree, layout: 'tabs' is a group. What is missing is almost entirely the drag gestures that move things between them.

Dragging content out: what actually happens

Reported as "the content isn't dropped where I placed it, it goes to the bottom as a new row". Measured, the rule is narrower than that, and worth stating exactly because it is not a tab bug at all.

The two root causes, found by tracing the commit. First, a cross-board drop computed the target board's displaced tiles and then dropped them on the floor: the batch carried the arriving tile's cell but not the push it caused, so the next rebuild read the pre-drop cells, found the arriving tile overlapping them and pushed it to the bottom. The ghost had been right all along; the commit ignored it. Second, the search for a landing cell read changed:false as "refused", but that answer also means "it is already there" — so once the tile was parked on the nearest legal cell, the next pointer move re-tried that cell, read the no-op as a refusal and knocked the tile to a farther one, oscillating for the whole drag. Both were invisible to every behaviour gate because both boards ended up self-consistent.

VS Code never relocates a drop. It shows you the zone you are over, and dropping there either joins that group or splits it — the editor cannot "not fit". A grid can, so the kit needs a deliberate answer: refuse the drop visibly the way a full board already does, shrink the tile to the free width, or push the row below down to make room. Quietly landing at the bottom is the one option that surprises people, and it is what happens today.

Two defects the probe found

FIXED in 0.4.28 — a non-container child of a tab container is orphaned. The tab system treats only children carrying widgets as pages, so a plain widget among them is never positioned: it paints at the board origin at its 100×60 placeholder size. Measured: 'loose' at x=0, 100×60, while the two real pages tabbed correctly. The same happens when every child is a plain widget: no strip is painted at all and the widget is stranded. The plan always said a plain child should become a one-widget page; the code does not do it yet. Until it does, this is a silent corruption of the author's layout, and it is the first thing to fix.
CLOSED in 0.4.30 — collapse on empty. A container whose last page leaves closes in the same undoable step; a one-page container keeps its one-tab bar, as a one-editor VS Code group does.
CLOSED in 0.4.32 — an empty PAGE closes too. When a page's last widget leaves (dragged out, moved into another board, removed, made a tab of its own) the page and its tab go, and an emptied container with them, in the same undoable step. This is what makes a one-widget page behave like "the widget is the tab" while a page holding several behaves like a board. Measured: Sales (one widget) → its widget dragged out → left strip Sales/Margin → Margin; a one-page group's widget dragged out → the group closed; both undo.

Against the field: the three most-used docking libraries, and the two closest to a dashboard

By weekly downloads the docking layout managers are Dockview (~117k, VS Code-shaped, framework-agnostic), FlexLayout (~65k, Caplin's React model, JSON-first) and GoldenLayout (~35k, the classic rows/columns/stacks). Neither of the two things closest to our product is a docking library: gridstack is the grid half (nested sub-grids, cross-grid drags, no tabs at all) and DevExpress Dashboard ships a Tab Container whose pages hold items and groups but may not nest. What the field says we missed. Beyond the four drop gaps already on the list (edge splits, sections into groups, split pages adopting, a nested strip inside the source container), every docking library ships a close button on the tab, an overflow menu once the strip is full, maximize / restore on a group, a lock on a group or a tab, and hooks that can veto a drop before the overlay is drawn — none of which we have. Dockview and GoldenLayout also handle touch by long-press; our 4 px threshold would tear a tab out on the first scroll. Dockview's external drag-in and gridstack's dragIn both let a new thing from a palette land on a strip or group; our palette lands on boards only. Floating groups and popout windows are in all three docking libraries and are the one family I would leave out on purpose: a dashboard is a page, not an IDE. DevExpress's one idea worth copying, "Display Item as Page", we already do — a one-widget page wears the widget's title.

The build order this suggests. (1) Edge splits, root docking and the nested-strip fix, which complete the drop model the three docking libraries share. (2) Close button, overflow menu, maximize / restore and per-group / per-tab lock — the strip's chrome, cheap and universal. (3) Sections into groups, split pages adopting, palette-to-strip — the grid-native half nobody else has to solve. (4) Drop hooks with a veto, touch long-press, and programmatic page moves — the embedder's surface. How the established libraries solve this

Three prior arts are worth copying from, and they split cleanly along the two halves of our model.

librarythe mechanism, concretelywhat to take
Lumino (JupyterLab's DockPanel)Thirteen named drop zones: root-all, root-top/left/right/bottom, widget-all, widget-top/left/right/bottom, widget-tab, invalid. The zone over a panel is computed by thirds: the centre third of both axes is widget-all, which appends as a tab; otherwise the nearest edge by scaled distance, which splits that way. A dedicated widget-tab zone fires when the pointer is within the tab bar's own height of the top edge, and inserts at that tab index. Root edges use fixed thresholds — 12 px at the top, 40 px on the other three — to dock against the whole panel.The zone model wholesale, including the numbers. Thirds are more forgiving than the fifth I sketched, and the separate tab-bar zone is what makes precise ordering possible.
Dockview (explicitly VS Code-shaped)Five target kinds: between two tabs, the empty end of a tab list, merge into a group, a position around a group, and magnet positions against the container edges. Ships hooks — onWillDragPanel, onWillDragGroup, onWillDrop, onWillShowOverlay — so a host can veto or reshape a drop, and a dropOverlayModel per target for size and activation thresholds. The insight that resolves the mode matrix: every docking library above is a proportional tree. A pane always has room because everything shares a percentage, so "it does not fit" cannot happen and relocation is never needed. Our kit is both things at once: split is that proportional tree, and grid is a cell board that genuinely can be full. So we should not pick one rule. Take Lumino's zones for the split side, where they were designed to live, and gridstack's push-or-swap for the grid side, where the cells are. The only mode that still needs an explicit refusal is fit with squeeze: false, because that is the one board that can truly have no room — and refusing is already what it does.

What the drag round actually costs

The three gestures you asked for — move a whole group, move one tab inside a group, pull a tab out into its own group — are one mechanism, not three. Each is a drop with a source and a target zone.

  1. Make a section draggable at all. Today it is a locked slab, which is why moveTo refuses. This is the largest piece and it is not tab-specific: it changes what a section is on the board. Doing it unlocks moving a plain section too, which the kit has never allowed.
  2. A drop-zone overlay over the panel under the pointer, using a fifth of the body (Dockview's activation size — Lumino's thirds from 0.4.33 to 0.4.41, until the user, putting a page back into its panel, found two thirds of it read as "beside"): the middle of both axes combines, an edge band splits that way, and a band the height of the strip inserts at a tab index. Root edges get fixed thresholds for docking against the whole board.
  3. Two commands, both undoable in one step: combine converts the target section into a tab container if it is not one and moves the dropped panel in as a page; tear out removes a page, wraps it back into a plain section, and drops it into the board or a new split pane.
  4. Collapse on empty so a container with one page becomes a plain section again, and one with none disappears.
  5. Reorder within the bar, which is the cheap one: a drag along the strip permuting the pages array. Lumino's widget-tab zone gives this for free once the overlay exists.
  6. Drop hooks in dockview's shape (onWillDrop, onWillShowOverlay) so an embedder can veto a drop or restyle the overlay instead of us hard-coding every policy.
  7. The overflow chevron, so a group with many pages stays usable.

Order matters here. Steps 1 and 4 are prerequisites for the gesture feeling right, and step 1 touches the board's model of a section rather than the tab code. I would ship it as its own round with the section-drag gates rewritten first, then the drop zones on top, rather than bolting the gesture onto locked slabs.
