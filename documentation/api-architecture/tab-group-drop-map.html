The Tab Group Drop Map

Every point over the fluid board’s side panel, on both layouts, sampled with an independent drag of the NPS widget: hold, read what the kit shows, release, measure what it did, undo, and check the board came back exactly. What was shown is what happened at every point.

A tab group answers a widget the same four ways on a grid board and on a split board: its header makes a tab, its page takes the widget, its outer fifth puts the widget beside it, and the band above its frame puts the widget above it. The split board says it in its own language — a pane and an insertion line instead of a cell and a pushed neighbour — but the map is the same map.

The previous version of this report, measured on 0.4.60, found the header honest and everything around it wrong: a fifth of the body meant “above the whole panel”, using it shoved the panel a row, and the tab target stayed behind. Eleven releases later the header is still honest and is now reachable at hand speed, “above” is above, under the header is the page, and the split board — which had none of this — has all of it, takes widgets out of pages, and grows a full section for a drop.

Two boards, one map

  • A new tab. The strip’s 30 pixels, full width, plus nine of stickiness once your hand is on it. A hand that crosses the strip between two pointer events and stops within a strip’s height of it is on the tabs too.
  • Into the page. Everything under the header that is not a fifth or the bottom band — including the first 30 pixels under the strip, which used to mean “above”. The page’s grey placeholder is the promise on both boards.
  • Beside, above, below. The outer fifths, a 30 px band above the frame, a 30 px band at the bottom. On the grid the panel gives way live; on the split board a line on the pane’s edge says where the new pane goes.
  • The board, or nothing. Off the panel: a plain cell, or off the board altogether.

The header, at hand speed

A strip is 30 pixels tall and a hand covers 40 to 80 pixels between two pointer events, so a probe that crawls a pixel a frame sees a header that a hand steps over. The segment the hand travelled is tested now, on both boards. Measured on the deployed build, coming down from above the panel:

Out of a page, onto the split board

On 0.4.69 a widget dragged out of the Filters page in split mode got no line and no placeholder anywhere on the board, the ghost dimmed, and every release snapped home: the split board refused to adopt. Now it takes the widget as a pane where the line shows, and a full section grows a row for it — the row coming from the panes above it, since a pane’s height is a share of its column.

Eight frames

How it got here

  1. 0.4.60The strip joined the zone walk, read where the panel rests. The loop that flickered between a tab and above closed.
  2. 0.4.61–63The top and bottom bands stopped moving the panel while held, became a fixed 30 px, and were painted as lanes.
  3. 0.4.65–66“Above” moved above the frame. The lanes and the deferral went: the bands push live again and the ordinary placeholder is the whole feedback.
  4. 0.4.67–68A hand is not a crawl: the segment it travelled is tested for a strip it stepped over; under the strip is the page; the band above gives its corners to the sides; the inset under the strip is the page’s.
  5. 0.4.69The split board runs the same zone walk for a widget over a tab group: strip, page, fifth, above.
  6. 0.4.70The split board adopts: a widget out of a page becomes a pane where the line shows; a palette chip goes through the same zones.
  7. 0.4.71A full section pane grows a row for a widget, the row coming from its column.

Measured on grafloria.com, Chromium 1440 × 1400, light. Each sample is an independent gesture: press, one pointer move to the target (a target above the strip is approached from above, the way a hand reaches it), read, release, measure, undo, compare the board to rest byte for byte. Sampling by continuous sweep was tried in the first version of this report and thrown away: the intermediate moves set zones on the way. The hand-speed journeys use one pointer event per step. Sources: demos/e2e/drop-map.mjs, the split observation probes, and the kit lab (L95, L103–L113).
